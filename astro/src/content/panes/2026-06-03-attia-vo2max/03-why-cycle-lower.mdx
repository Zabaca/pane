# 🚴 vs 🏃 Why the bike gives a *lower* VO2max

<Callout type="warning" title="Flipping the framing">
It's the other way around: the <strong>treadmill reads higher</strong>; the
<strong>cycle reads ~10–20% lower</strong>. So the question is really: why does
cycling under-measure your true aerobic ceiling? One word: <strong>muscle
mass</strong>.
</Callout>

## The core reason: how much muscle is working

VO2max = how much oxygen your body can consume at peak. That's set by central
supply (heart pumping blood) **and** peripheral demand (muscle pulling oxygen out
of that blood). The test can only show a number as high as the working muscle
*asks* for.

- **Treadmill** (running/incline walking): you move and support your whole body
  weight. Legs, glutes, trunk, postural and stabilizing muscles all fire — a large
  total muscle mass demanding oxygen. The limit you hit is your **heart/lungs**.
- **Cycle**: work is concentrated in the quads and hips, non-weight-bearing. Less
  total muscle is recruited, so peak oxygen demand is lower — and you often stop
  from **burning legs before your cardiovascular system maxes out**.

  T["Treadmill"] --> TM["recruits more muscle mass<br/>(whole-body, weight-bearing)"]

  C["Cycle"] --> CM["recruits less muscle mass<br/>(quad-dominant, seated)"]

## The contributing factors

| Factor | Treadmill | Cycle | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active muscle mass | High (whole body) | Lower (quad-dominant) | less O₂ demand on bike |
| Weight-bearing | Yes — moves body mass | No — seated | less total work on bike |
| Local muscle fatigue | Spread out | Concentrated in quads | bike test ends early |
| Intramuscular pressure | Lower | High in quads → impedes local blood flow | limits O₂ delivery to legs |
| Familiarity | Most people walk/run | Less practiced (non-cyclists) | sub-max effort on bike |

<Callout type="info" title="The tell-tale sign">
On a bike, people usually quit saying "my legs are done," not "I can't breathe."
That local quad fatigue caps the test before the heart reaches its ceiling — which
is exactly why the *central*-limited treadmill yields a higher number.
</Callout>

## The nuance: it depends who you are

The gap **shrinks for trained cyclists** — their legs are conditioned enough that
the local limiter recedes and the test gets closer to central max. For everyone
else, expect the bike to undercount. Hence the paper's practical rule: pick a
modality, and only compare a result to **same-modality** norms.

<Callout type="success" title="One-line answer">
The treadmill recruits more muscle and is limited by the heart/lungs (your real
ceiling); the bike concentrates work in the quads, where local fatigue ends the
test early — so it reads ~10–20% lower.
</Callout>
