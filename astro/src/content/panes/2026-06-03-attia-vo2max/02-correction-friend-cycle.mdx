# 🔧 Correction: my earlier bands were too high (and the wrong modality)

I checked response 01's numbers against the PDF you gave me — Kaminsky et al.,
*Mayo Clinic Proceedings* 2017, the **FRIEND Registry cycle-ergometry** reference
standards. My illustrative bands don't match it. Here's the honest reconciliation.

<Callout type="error" title="The headline error">
My chart showed a 40-year-old man's bands as Low 30 → Elite 60. But in this
paper, even the <strong>90th percentile</strong> for men aged 40–49 is only
<strong>37.1 mL/kg/min</strong>, and the median (50th) is <strong>27.1</strong>.
My "Elite 60" sat off the top of the entire table.
</Callout>

## Why the gap: cycle vs treadmill

This is the crux. The paper is explicit:

> "maximum oxygen consumption values (VO₂max) are approximately **10% to 20%
> lower when using a cycle ergometer compared with a treadmill**."

Most popular VO2max norms — and the figures Attia tends to cite — come from
**treadmill** testing (the earlier FRIEND treadmill paper, Kaminsky 2015). So my
numbers were both **too high** *and* implicitly treadmill-flavored, then I never
said which modality. Comparing them to this cycle table is apples-to-oranges on
top of just being inflated.

## Translating to treadmill (closer to the usual numbers)

The paper includes a "tm-adj-R" row = **85% of the FRIEND treadmill reference**.
For men 40–49 that adjusted value is **33.0**, which implies the treadmill
reference is roughly:

<Callout type="info" title="Back-of-envelope">
33.0 ÷ 0.85 ≈ <strong>~39 mL/kg/min as the treadmill median (50th) for men
40–49</strong>. So on a treadmill, ~39 is <em>average</em>, not "below average"
as I'd labeled 38. My categories were mislabeled by roughly one full band.
</Callout>

So a fairer treadmill-based picture for a 40-something man: median ≈ high-30s,
"good" (≈80th) low-to-mid 40s, and the 50–60 range I'd called "high/elite" is
genuinely **well-trained-to-athlete** territory — not a routine target.

## What the paper *does* confirm

- **~10% decline per decade** — the paper measured 10.0% (men) and 10.3% (women)
  for relative VO2max across the 20s→70s. My response 01 had this right.
- **Large sex difference** — men ~30% higher than women here. Right direction.
- **Steep drop with age** — cycle median for men falls 41.9 (20–29) → 19.5
  (70–79). Confirmed.
