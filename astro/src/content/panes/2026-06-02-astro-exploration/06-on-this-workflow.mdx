# 🪞 Honestly? It's good for *some* of what I say, not all

You asked for my real take, so here's the unvarnished version — where this shines
and where it fights me.

## Where it's clearly better than the terminal

<Callout type="success" title="Structured, durable answers">
The terminal is a scrolling log — answers vanish upward and can't be revisited
cleanly. Panes are addressable, grouped, and survive the session. The "session
grouping" idea only makes sense *because* answers are now artifacts, not stream.
</Callout>

- **Showing, not just telling.** A diagram, a chart, a highlighted table reads in
  seconds; the same thing as ASCII or prose is worse. This response has a mermaid
  diagram below precisely because the point is visual.
- **It composes.** Components mean I can reach for the right container (callout,
  card, chart) instead of flattening everything into one voice of markdown.
- **It's revisitable.** You can scroll back to response 03 next week. Terminal
  scrollback is hostile to that.

## Where it genuinely costs us

<Callout type="warning" title="Latency & friction">
A terminal reply is instant. A pane is: write file → hot-reload → you switch to
the browser → read. For a quick yes/no that's pure overhead. The medium only
pays off when the content has structure worth the round-trip.
</Callout>

- **It's one-way right now.** You type here, I answer there. There's no input
  *from* the pane back to me yet — so it's a richer monologue, not a dialogue.
  That's the missing half (the round-trip we flagged in response 02).
- **Context-switching tax.** Your eyes have to leave the place you're typing.
- **Failure modes are quieter.** If a pane errors, you might not notice as fast as
  a terminal error.

## My honest heuristic

<Mermaid chart={`flowchart TD
  Q{"What am I answering?"}
  Q -->|"quick fact / yes-no / status"| T["Terminal — instant, no switch"]
  Q -->|"structured, visual, or worth keeping"| P["Pane — diagram, chart, grouped"]
  Q -->|"a decision needing your input"| B["Both eventually:<br/>pane shows options,<br/>round-trip captures choice"]
`} />

So my opinion: **this is the right primary surface for substantive, visual, or
durable answers — which is most of what this project is about — but it shouldn't
be dogmatic.** A trivial confirmation doesn't deserve a file and a context switch.

The thing that would make me fully sold is closing the loop: when a pane can ask
*you* something and get the answer back, this stops being "nicer output" and
becomes an actual interface. Until then it's a very good broadcast channel.

<Callout type="info" title="One practical worry">
As sessions grow, write-file-per-response is great for humans but I should be
careful not to bury a one-line answer in ceremony. The discipline is: match the
weight of the pane to the weight of the answer.
</Callout>
